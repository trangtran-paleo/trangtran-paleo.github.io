Trang Tran

Paleoclimatology  ·  Organic Geochemistry  ·  University of Arizona

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Geosciences at the University of Arizona. My research focuses on reconstructing past climate variability using organic geochemical proxies and integrating these records with climate model simulations.

I investigate the drivers of past climate variability — particularly sea surface temperatures and hydroclimate — through proxies such as TEX86, UK′37, and leaf-wax hydrogen isotopes preserved in marine sediments.

My work bridges proxy reconstruction and model–data comparison, asking how well climate simulations capture the range of variability recorded in geological archives. I am particularly drawn to intervals of significant climate transition, from the late Miocene through the Pleistocene.

In academic publications, my name appears as T. Tran.
