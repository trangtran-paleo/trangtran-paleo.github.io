Ongoing · Dissertation Chapter

Long-Term Climate Evolution · 10 Ma to 1 Ma

My primary dissertation project investigates sea surface temperature and hydroclimate changes from 10 Ma to 1 Ma — spanning the late Miocene through the early Pleistocene. I combine organic geochemical records from marine sediment cores with climate model outputs to characterize the structure and pacing of long-term climate evolution during this interval of global cooling and the onset of Northern Hemisphere glaciation.

Dissertation Chapter

Late Pleistocene Hydroclimate Variability

I reconstruct past precipitation variability using leaf-wax hydrogen isotopes (δD) from marine sediments and compare these proxy records with climate model simulations. This project addresses how tropical and subtropical hydroclimate responded to orbital forcing and glacial–interglacial cycles during the late Pleistocene.

Dissertation Chapter

Sea Surface Temperature Reconstructions

I use organic geochemical proxies — including TEX86 and UK′37 — to reconstruct past sea surface temperature variability from marine sediment archives. A key component of this work involves calibration uncertainty, diagenetic assessment, and integration of multi-proxy SST estimates to improve reconstruction fidelity.
